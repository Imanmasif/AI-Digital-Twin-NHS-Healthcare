# Cardiovascular Use Cases

## Heart failure

The paper uses heart failure as a principal example because patient trajectories and treatment responses vary substantially.

The proposed digital twin could combine ejection fraction and comorbidities from clinical records, MRI or echocardiography, laboratory measures such as BNP and creatinine, and wearable measurements such as activity and heart-rate variability. The twin could then support short-term risk prediction and what-if comparisons for treatment changes, including medication titration or device interventions.

The intended output is clinician-facing decision support rather than autonomous treatment. The paper identifies readmission, mortality, functional status and quality-of-life outcomes as examples of clinical endpoints that would need to be evaluated in a real deployment.

## Atrial fibrillation

The second example concerns personalised planning for atrial fibrillation ablation. The proposed approach combines cardiac imaging and electrophysiological information to build a patient-specific model. AI could then be used to estimate procedure outcomes or recurrence risk and to compare possible ablation strategies.

The paper presents this as a potential application of digital twins, not as evidence that a validated patient-specific ablation system has been deployed through this repository.

## Cross-cutting considerations

The paper identifies potential benefits in personalised treatment, risk prediction, patient engagement and system-level efficiency, while emphasising that interoperability, evidence, equity, workflow integration and governance remain major requirements for real-world adoption.
